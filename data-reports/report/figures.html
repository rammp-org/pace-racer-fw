1 · Motor identification and loop tuning

The autotune chain: measure R and L electrically, measure J mechanically, place the loop poles from the model, then verify with progressively tighter step responses.

1.1 · R and L from voltage steps

The rl command applies an open-loop d-axis voltage step at θ=0 (current PI bypassed) and records iα every 50 µs. Four levels are fitted at once:

  • R from the slope of Vss vs Iss across levels — the inverter's deadtime + device-drop error is nearly constant, so it lands in the intercept instead of corrupting R.
  • L from the exponential rise: ln(1 − i/Iss) = −t/τ, with L = R·τ.

Results: R = 0.1659 Ω phase = 0.332 Ω line-line (bench DMM said 0.323 Ω — 3% apart). Voltage intercept 0.305 V = the deadtime/device error the slope method rejects. L = 395 µH phase (log-linear fit; the crude 63% crossing gave 417 µH). Current-loop gains follow directly: for bandwidth ωc = 2π·300 Hz, kp = ωcL = 0.745 V/A and ki = ωcR = 313 V/(A·s).

1.2 · Inertia from a torque step

A fixed iq = 3 A (T = Kt·iq = 1.86 N·m, Kt = 0.62 N·m/A measured against the rotary torque sensor) from standstill; J = Kt·iq / (dω/dt) on the linear region after breakaway. This J is drag-inclusive — bearing and brake-remanence drag lower the net torque — which is exactly the plant the speed loop drives.

Result: dω/dt = 161 rpm/s at 2.87 A → J = 105.7 m·kg·m².

1.3 · Velocity loop: pole placement, then progressive verification

Plant model from the measurements above: dω/dt = K·iq with K = Kt/J·60/2π = 56.0 (rpm/s)/A. With PI control the closed loop is s² + K·kp·s + K·ki = 0, so choosing (ωn, ζ) sets both gains: kp = 2ζωn/K, ki = ωn²/K. Each run steps the setpoint 0 → 50 rpm:

2 · Disturbance rejection — torque step at 100 rpm

Spinning at 100 rpm under encoder commutation, the particle brake applies a 2.6 N·m step and releases it. Capture at 250 Hz.

Result: steady 100 rpm; the 2.6 N·m step dips speed to 89.5 rpm (10 rpm sag) with recovery inside a second; release overshoots to 124 rpm and settles back to 100 rpm. (Re-run 2026-08-20 on the rebuilt encoder mount — the original trace, whose tail collapsed as the first mount failed mid-run, lives in the run archive.) Brake-command vs capture alignment is ±0.3 s (host serial latency).

3 · Continuously varying load

Same 100 rpm hold while the brake follows two cycles of a slow sinusoidal load, ≈0.3-2 N·m nominal with a 10 s period. Load and drive torque share one axis in N·m.

Result: 99.9 ± 0.7 rpm across the whole sweep (worst excursion 2.5 rpm); drive torque tracks the load command in phase. The gap between the two torque curves is the brake's real-vs-nominal map plus friction — the nominal map reads low after any high-excitation event (remanence).

4 · Lock-rotor thermal steady state

d-axis current hold (rotor parked by alignment, θ=0 — worst case: the same FET pair carries the current continuously). LM75 sensors on the board, hottest of four plotted. Each step holds until the rise flattens (<0.4 °C/min) or 5 min.

Copper check: dissipation at each step is ≈ 1.5·R·I² = 6 W @ 5 A, 25 W @ 10 A, 56 W @ 15 A (motor windings; FET conduction adds on top).

The 20 A step is the finding, not a data point (baseline): with the board pre-heated to ~41 °C by the 15 A hold, stepping to 20 A DC through a single FET pair tripped the DRV8353's VDS overcurrent backstop (fault 0x0620, latched) within 1.5 s — Rds(on) derates with temperature until 20 A rides the 0.10 V (vds 4) threshold. The practical sustained lock-rotor ceiling at this VDS setting is 15 A warm / 20 A only from cold. Note this is the θ=0 worst case; a rotating or commutating drive shares the heat across all six FETs.

4.1 · With vs without heatsinks

Re-run 2026-08-21 after adding heatsinking on the FET thermal-via pads on the board's back. Same protocol; the y-axis is ΔT above each run's own starting temperature, so different room days compare fairly. The re-run used vds 5, which is why its 20 A step could run where the baseline's tripped the 0.10 V VDS backstop within 1.5 s.

Result: At the board sensors, the heatsinks change little: 11.4 → 11.1 °C rise at 15 A — expected, because the LM75s measure board temperature and the dissipated power is unchanged; the heatsink's job is the FET junction-to-ambient path, which these sensors can't see. The junction-side evidence: the 20 A DC hold — which tripped the VDS backstop within 1.5 s on the bare board — now runs to a full plateau at 48.1 °C (206 s, ΔT 19.1 °C). Attribution is shared with the raised VDS threshold (0.10 → 0.20 V), so this shows the 20 A hold is now practical, not that heatsinks alone made it so. The phase-C current clamp read 9.4 A against the expected |−I/2| = 10 A — the board's current calibration confirmed within 6% by an independent instrument (clamp orientation flips the sign).

5 · Torque vs current — encoder commutation is linear

eiq staircase with the rotor held by the particle brake, torque read from the sensor's 0–10 V output on the scope over LAN. The commutation offset came from a torque-peak sweep: constant 8 A while stepping the offset through 360° — torque peaks at the true angle, and since nothing moves, gear dynamics can't contaminate the measurement.

The sensor's analog output reports magnitude only, so the curve is |cos| with sharp nulls ±90° from the true angle — the nulls locate the offset to a degree or two.

Result: torque per amp holds 0.58–0.64 N·m/A from 4 to 16 A — flat within ~9%, mean Kt = 0.61 N·m/A, matching the dyno constant (0.62). Under hall commutation the same regime collapsed to 0.29 N·m/A (§4 of the campaign report), and a stalled power run today gave the same number at maximum current: 30 A hall-commutated stall delivered 11.6 N·m = 0.39 N·m/A vs the encoder's 0.58. Cross-check: at 16 A the sensor's own display read 9.3–9.4 N·m vs 9.34 from the analog path. The 18 A step ended the staircase when commanded torque exceeded the brake's static hold — the breakaway spiked the 30 A software guard, which unloaded cleanly.

6 · Maximum power at 150 rpm — 514 W bus

Hall-commutated 150 rpm hold while the 200 N·m particle brake ramps; bus power measured at the 48 V supply. Current ceiling raised to 30 A target / 35 A trip for this run.

The final sample (t=64.3 s, 1 W) is the protection stack ending the run: the 35 A guard unloaded the bridge to zero and coasted, so bus draw collapsed while the wheel kept spinning on its own inertia (rpm still 151 — that row's iq is a stale mid-unload sample).

Result: 514 W peak bus power, above 500 W for ~11 s at 149–151 rpm with 17.0 N·m on the sensor (≈267 W mechanical + ≈230 W motor copper at ~30 A + drive losses). FETs peaked at 50 °C. The run ended when the speed loop overshot to 35 A chasing a brake fluctuation and the software guard unloaded gently — protection by design, no hardware faults. At 150 rpm the power is torque-limited by the 30 A ceiling; the §4 thermal data says the board itself has headroom well beyond this.

6.1 · Where the power goes

At the sustained >500 W operating point (mean of the three samples: 510 W bus, 30.6 A, 17.0 N·m at 150 rpm): mechanical output is T·ω; motor copper is 1.5·R·I² with the measured R (this is heat in the motor windings, not the board); the drive electronics (FET conduction + switching) and iron/friction take the small remainder.

System efficiency at this point: 52% — a property of the operating corner, not the hardware: torque costs current (copper heat ∝ I²) while output power needs speed, so a max-torque / low-speed point is inherently copper-dominated. The same 17 N·m at 400 rpm would put mechanical output near 710 W against the same ~240 W of losses (≈75%) — §7 later confirmed it: 73% at 848 W / 400 rpm and 78% at 1049 W / 483 rpm. The drive electronics themselves burn only ~9 W (2% of bus).

6.3 · Board temperature vs bus power

Read with care: these are transient temperatures during 60–90 s ramps, not steady state — §4 showed the board needs 2–4 minutes to plateau, so each point is still rising when sampled. The honest statement: at 510 W bus the board reached ~50 °C and was climbing roughly 1 °C per 4 s; extrapolating with the §4 thermal resistance (~5 °C/W on ~13 W of board dissipation) puts the steady-state board temperature near 60–65 °C at this power in a 25 °C room — inside the 80 °C guard with margin.

7 · The 1 kW chase — a torque wall at 400 rpm, crossed on speed

2026-08-26/27. Hall-commutated speed holds under stepped brake loads; soft trip raised 35→45 A (below), vl 26 V, Iset 24 A. Torque from the inline sensor, independent phase-C current peaks from a 100 A clamp.

Why the trip moved: the 08-20 campaign’s intermittent over-current aborts reported 49–52 A on phase C, but a 100 A current clamp on that phase, armed to trigger at 35 A, never fired — reproduced three times. The spikes are conversion artifacts, not current (the reads sat beyond the ADC’s +51/−39 A rail only because the reconstructed phase can read to 2× rail). With the trip at 45 A the ladders below logged zero phantom trips while the clamp confirmed real peaks stayed ≤ 29 A. Caveat: at 45 A the trip is blind to negative excursions on the measured pair (rail −39 A); the DRV VDS OCP stays as the hardware layer.

7.1 · The 400 rpm attempts

The ladder ended one load step past 847.7 W with DRV8353 fault 0x0628 (VDS OCP, high-side A+B) — the second of the day: an earlier coarser ladder peaked at 790.8 W and faulted on a ~2 N·m load step. The second came on a gentle ~0.7 N·m increment from a steady 847.7 W, which rules out load-step transients: this is the VDS threshold (0.20 V ≈ 58–74 A cold) derating with FET temperature into legitimate peak current. Both faults struck with the board at 57–60 °C after ~2 min of loaded laddering; the clamp read 28–29 A real peaks at the time. Brake remanence, quantified: identical brake excitation produced 232 W of load on a fresh coil, 340 W later the same afternoon, and 400 W after a high-excitation event — a 72% spread, which is why §8 closes the loop on measured torque instead.

Efficiency at the 848 W point: 73% (14.7 N·m × 400 rpm = 616 W mechanical) — versus 52% at §6’s 510 W / 150 rpm corner. Same hardware, same currents, 20 points better: power bought with speed instead of torque skips the I² copper tax. This is the curve §6.1 predicted.

Control-loop timing, exonerated: every rung of every ladder logged late=0.1% and cmax ≤ 57 µs against the 50 µs PWM period — at 848 W the FOC task ran exactly as it does idle. The earlier “late stuck at 5%” scare was a reading error: the s/sq stats reset on every read, so a first read after boot averages in the zero-cal and arm work. Pure-idle windows read 0.1%. There is no firmware timing limit in evidence.

7.2 · 1049 W at 500 rpm — the speed route

Fixed speed means power only grows through torque, and torque costs current: crossing ~850 W at 400 rpm demands ~29 A peaks, inside the derated VDS band. Raising speed buys power without current: the hrun command clamp went ±400 → ±520 rpm (bemf ~21 V + IR ~3 V fits the 26 V ceiling), and on 2026-08-27 a cold-start ladder at 500 rpm walked straight through the fault band — phase-C peaks stayed near 21 A where the 400 rpm attempts needed 29 A.

Bench notes

  • Torque sensor scale: the analog path read 10× low vs the sensor's display (10:1 probe on a 1× scope channel); all torque here uses the corrected 200 N·m/V, cross-checked against the display at 16 A.
  • Brake: a 200 N·m-class unit — the early low-voltage map was just the bottom of its curve. Its torque at a given voltage rises after high-excitation events (remanence), which moves the stall edge between runs; the harness also re-asserts the supply channel's current limit on every write (its 0.1 A power-on default silently starves the coil).
  • Encoder mount: the first mount degraded and broke on 2026-08-19; figures 5 and the fig-2 re-run used the rebuilt mount, whose offset held within a few degrees through max-torque events.
  • Console wedge (watch item): the USB-JTAG console intermittently goes mute — chip cold boots don't clear it, USB re-enumeration does, pointing at the host driver instance. Harness mitigations: stream-quiet around port open/close and a software replug via hub port power-cycling.
  • Phantom over-current, closed (08-26): the intermittent 49-52 A phase-C trip reports were proven phantom against a 100 A clamp (never above 29 A real, 3× reproduced); soft trip raised 35→45 A, zero phantom trips since. The gate-drive scope session happened: ~322 ns dead-time, zero overlap samples, no shoot-through.
  • VDS OCP derating is the current power wall (08-26): two real 0x0628 faults (high-side A+B) at 850-950 W attempts with the board at 57-60 °C — the 0.20 V threshold’s amp value falls with hot Rdson into real ~29 A peaks. A cold start did NOT clear it (third fault at 44 °C) — resolved by the 500 rpm speed route instead; see §7.2.
  • Shutdown order matters: stopping the motor while the brake is still energized fired a VDS fault after an otherwise clean 1 kW run (hard decel + back-EMF). The harness now releases the brake, waits, then stops the motor.
  • Sampler timing at speed (watch item): late is 0.1% at ≤400 rpm but a steady 4.9% at 500 rpm (cmax ≤46 µs, no overruns). Fine today; bounds the next speed increment.
  • Firmware 2026-08-27: speed-command clamp ±400 → ±520 rpm; DRV VDS level 6 (0.30 V) exposed on the console but unused — the 1 kW point was reached with the stock 0.20 V threshold.
  • Stats counters reset on read: s/sq print-and-zero; a single read after boot mixes cal/arm work into the late percentage. Take a throwaway read, wait, read again.
  • The stream task can block silently if an LM75/I2C read hangs — the thermal guard is blind in that state (VM cycle recovers).
